Privacy Policy

This privacy policy applies to the Run Story app (hereby referred to as "Application") for mobile devices that was created by Finish Line Labs, LLC (hereby referred to as "Service Provider") as a Free service. This service is intended for use "AS IS".


What information does the Application obtain and how is it used?

The Application does not directly collect personally identifiable information. To function, it relies on the following:

  • Connected fitness account. You choose one fitness service to connect — Strava, Garmin Connect, or Apple Health — and the Application uses it to obtain your activity data (distance, time, route, heart rate, splits, photos, and similar). Only one service is connected at a time. Each is described below, because where your data travels differs between them.
  • Strava. You authenticate using Strava's OAuth. The Application receives an access token, stored in encrypted storage on your device, and uses it to fetch your activity data from Strava on your behalf. Activity data is cached locally on your device so the Application can display it offline. A webhook service receives a notification from Strava whenever one of your activities is created, changed, or deleted, and stores a record of it on the Service Provider's servers (Amazon DynamoDB, hosted in AWS US West). That record contains your Strava athlete identifier, the activity's identifier, the time of the change, the kind of change, and — for edits — which fields changed, which can include an activity's title. The contents of your activities (route, distance, heart rate, photos) are not sent to or stored on the Service Provider's servers; the Application fetches those directly from Strava and caches them on your device. These notification records are retained rather than deleted after they are delivered. See "Data retention and deletion" below.
  • Garmin Connect. You authenticate using Garmin's OAuth. Unlike Strava, Garmin does not allow activities to be requested on demand — it pushes them to the Service Provider's servers as they are recorded. This means that, for Garmin users, activity data is stored on the Service Provider's servers (Amazon DynamoDB, hosted in AWS US East), including route coordinates, split and lap data, heart-rate samples, and the name of the recording device. Your Garmin authorization tokens are also stored on those servers, because the servers must be able to receive data on your behalf while the Application is closed. Activity data received from Garmin is kept so that it can be re-synced to you — Garmin does not allow an application to request your past activities, so this stored copy is the only way your history can be restored if you reinstall the Application or set up a new device. It is deleted on request. See "Data retention and deletion" below.
  • Apple Health. On iOS, you may connect Apple Health instead. Workout data is read on your device through Apple's HealthKit framework and is used only to display and compose your graphics. Apple Health data is never transmitted to the Service Provider's servers and is never shared with any third party. It is never used for advertising or analytics.
  • Place names. To label where an activity started, the Application converts the first coordinate of your route into a place name. On iOS this uses Apple's geocoding service; on Android it uses the device's geocoding service and, for some activities, the OpenStreetMap Nominatim service. This means an activity's starting coordinate may be sent to those services. Results are cached on your device so the same location is not looked up repeatedly.
  • Advertising. The Application displays banner ads via Google AdMob. AdMob may collect device identifiers and ad-interaction data in accordance with Google's privacy policy. Ads can be removed by purchase.
  • Analytics. The Application uses Google Firebase Analytics to record anonymous, aggregate usage events (for example, which features are used) to help improve the Application.
  • Maps. The Application renders maps using the Google Maps SDK. Standard device and request information may be transmitted to Google in accordance with Google's privacy policy.
  • Notifications. If you opt in to the weekly or monthly summary notification, the Application schedules a local notification on your device. No notification content is sent through a remote server.

Does the Application collect precise real time location information of the device?

This Application does not access your device's location services and does not collect real-time location data. The only location information the Application handles is whatever is already attached to the activities you have recorded with your connected service, which you control through that service. As described above, an activity's starting coordinate may be sent to a geocoding service in order to display a place name.


Artificial intelligence and machine learning

The Application uses on-device machine learning for one feature: cutting the subject out of a background photo, so your stats can be layered behind you.

  • What it processes: only a photo you have chosen yourself as a background. It is not applied to your activity data, your location data, or your health data.
  • Where it runs: entirely on your device — using Apple's Vision framework on iOS, and Google's ML Kit on-device subject segmentation on Android. The photo is not uploaded anywhere for this. On Android, the model itself is delivered to your device by Google Play services; your photo is not sent in return.
  • What is kept: nothing. The cutout exists only for as long as you are editing, and the original photo stays where it was.

The Service Provider does not use your activity data, location data, or health data to train, retrain, or improve any artificial intelligence or machine learning model, and does not supply that data to any third-party or generative AI service. Data from a connected fitness service is used only to display your activities and to compose the graphics and videos you create.


Face Data (TrueDepth / ARKit Face Tracking)

On supported iOS devices, the Application offers an optional Augmented Reality (AR) feature that overlays your run statistics on a live camera view. This AR feature uses Apple's ARKit framework, which on devices equipped with the TrueDepth camera may access face-tracking information (face geometry and depth data) to position the AR overlay correctly in front of you.

What is collected: Face geometry and depth data provided by ARKit while the AR camera view is active.

Purpose: Used solely to position and render the AR stats overlay on the live camera image during the AR session. The data is never used to identify a user, build a profile, train any model, or for any purpose other than rendering the AR overlay.

Where it is stored / shared: Face data is processed entirely on your device, in memory, only while the AR session is running. It is never saved to disk, never transmitted off the device, never sent to the Service Provider's servers, and never shared with any third party (including Strava, Garmin, Apple, Google AdMob, Google Firebase, or Google Maps). When the AR session ends, the face data is discarded.

Retention: Zero. No face data persists beyond the active AR session.

Data retention and deletion

Activity data cached on your device is removed when you uninstall the Application.

For Strava, the notification records described above are kept on the Service Provider's servers after they are delivered to your device, so that an activity the Application has not yet collected — because the app was not opened, or was reinstalled — is not lost. They are automatically deleted 90 days after they are received. Expiring them costs nothing you cannot get back: the records only say that an activity changed, and the Application reads the activity itself from Strava directly. The contents of your activities are not held on those servers at all. You can also have every record held for your athlete identifier deleted sooner by emailing mike@finishlinelabs.info.

For Garmin Connect, activity data received from Garmin is kept on the Service Provider's servers so it can be re-synced to you. This is because Garmin does not allow an application to request your past activities: Garmin sends each activity once, as it is recorded, and it cannot be asked for again. The stored copy is therefore what allows your history to be restored to the Application if you reinstall it, sign in on a new device, or clear the Application's data. Without it, your earlier activities would be permanently unavailable to you.

It is kept for that purpose only. It is not used for advertising, not sold or shared, and not used to train any model.

There are two ways to disconnect, and they do different things.

Disconnecting inside the Application stops it syncing on that device and deletes your stored authorization tokens. Your activity data is kept, so that reconnecting later restores your history rather than starting you from empty.

Revoking the Application from Garmin Connect — in Garmin's own connected-applications settings — is treated as an instruction to stop holding your data, not only to stop receiving more of it. When Garmin notifies the Service Provider that you have revoked access, or that you have withdrawn permission for activity data, all activity data held for your account is deleted, along with your tokens. This is permanent: Garmin cannot re-send activities it has already sent, so a history deleted this way cannot be restored, even if you reconnect later.

You can have it deleted at any time. Email mike@finishlinelabs.info and the Service Provider will delete all activity data held for your account. Deletion is permanent, and because Garmin cannot re-send past activities, the deleted history cannot be recovered afterwards.


What are my opt-out rights?

You can stop all collection of information by the Application by uninstalling it through the standard uninstall process on your mobile device or via the mobile application marketplace. You can also revoke the Application's access to your connected service at any time: through Strava's "My Apps" settings, through Garmin Connect's connected-applications settings, or through the Health app's privacy settings on iOS.
